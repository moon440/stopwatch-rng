The Stopwatch RNG — a TempleOS port

Terry A. Davis built TempleOS from 2005 to 2017. Every random number in it comes from a stopwatch: a hardware counter folded into the running state on every draw, plus a "god" oracle whose entropy is the latency of your own keypresses. This page embeds his exact generator, transcribed 1:1 from the TempleOS kernel, compiled to a 968-byte WebAssembly module. No server, no framework, no network. Press keys, watch it react.

3. Truth about the stopwatch



  TempleOS read the CPU's GetTSC at gigahertz from Ring 0. A WebAssembly module
  cannot touch that register — sandbox — so this build imports a clock from the
  page that hosts it: performance.now at microsecond resolution. Modern browsers clamp that
  clock, sometimes hard (0.1 ms, or coarser in cold tabs). The clamp means the wasm
  stopwatch is *quieter* than Terry's machine, not different in nature: his counter also
  only moves meaningfully because his programs ran for a while between draws. Near-idle
  intervals starve the raw XOR, in his kernel and in this one. The Seed(n) button kills the
  stopwatch on purpose: that is TASKf_NONTIMER_RAND, the deterministic mode from
  Demo/RandDemo.HC. Each WebAssembly tab instance owns its state module-wide, like one
  HolyC task.
